An independent researcher is a researcher who conducts and publishes work **without institutional affiliation** — no post, no laboratory. The interesting question is not the status: it is **how this work becomes citable, verifiable and open to discussion** without the credit a priori that an institution confers. This page answers in two steps: the concrete infrastructure, then the analysis of the position itself.

<div class="resultat-phrase">

**The result in one sentence.** Publishing without an institution is now well equipped — DOIs, an ORCID identifier, preprints, indexing, journals — but the equipment does not manufacture recognition: without credit a priori, it is method that carries the burden of proof, and it does not replace the challenge from contradictors that an institution provides when it functions.

</div>

## The infrastructure: publishing outside the walls, a user's guide

Some of the tools below did not exist twenty years ago: ORCID dates from 2012, Zenodo from 2013. An independent researcher's minimal toolkit:

<div class="outils-grille">

1. **One DOI per text** — a DOI (*Digital Object Identifier*) is a permanent identifier that leads to the text even if its address changes. An open repository such as [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org) (operated by CERN) assigns one to each working paper, timestamps the deposit and keeps its successive versions; the publication date it displays, however, can be chosen by the depositor. The DOI makes the work **citable** in a stable way; it does not make it a peer-reviewed publication.
2. **A researcher identity** — [ORCID](https://orcid.org) provides a unique identifier, independent of any institution, that links works to their author in academic databases.
3. **Circulation through preprints** — a preprint is a paper circulated before, or without, review by a journal. Platforms such as SSRN (*Social Science Research Network*) circulate them without requiring an affiliation, after an integrity check (plagiarism, falsified data, false authorship claims, among others), which does not replace that review; their announcement by email to a discipline's subscribers depends on the platform editors' choice.
4. **Academic indexing** — clean metadata (title, authors, date, abstract) lets Google Scholar discover and link the work; keywords and classification codes (in economics, those of the *Journal of Economic Literature*) serve the indexes that read them.
5. **Journals remain open** — an author without a post can submit to them and publish in them; the article follows the same process as any other: peer review, that is, review by other researchers in the field.

</div>

None of this manufactures recognition — it comes, as for any researcher, through citation and discussion. And review does not always set the author aside: in single-anonymised review, the most common kind according to the publisher Elsevier, the reviewer sees the author's name and affiliation. For the same finance paper, one experiment counted 23% of reviewers recommending rejection when only a Nobel laureate's name was shown, 48% when the paper was anonymised, and 65% when only the name of an early-career researcher was shown (Huber et al., *PNAS*, 2022). But the infrastructure lowers the old entry barrier: **a piece of work can now exist publicly, dated and citable, without an institution's approval** — within each platform's rules.

## What the absence of an institution demands in return

Without credit a priori, it falls to method to carry the burden of proof. In practice:

<div class="exigences-liste">

- **dated, versioned texts with DOIs** — so that every claim has a stable source;
- **canonical definitions** — a fixed vocabulary, reused identically from one text to the next;
- **explicit criteria for refutation** — stating what, if observed, would invalidate the hypothesis;
- **an organised corpus** — a numbered series rather than scattered texts, so that the work can be cited as a programme.

</div>

This is the protocol the series of [*Anthropie Working Papers*](/en/serie-awp/) (AWP) sets itself, the corpus that serves as the concrete case for this page: nine numbered working papers, each in French and English, each version with its own Zenodo DOI and linked to the other; explicit refutation conditions in five of them (AWP-01, 05, 07, 08 and 09).

Method does not, for all that, replace what the institution, when it functions, provides. AWP-05 calls it "the forge": the regulated contradiction of seminars, defences and peer review, in which a thesis only stands if it has withstood its contradictors; without it, a hypothesis "may harden into conviction before it has been sufficiently tested".

## The position analysed: a margin that may be no accident {#the-position-analysed-the-margin-is-no-accident}

The [anthropic framework](/en/quest-ce-que-lanthropie/) offers a reading of this position — applied to the academic field itself. The working paper [AWP-05 — *Thinking Beyond the Walls*](/en/awp/awp-05/) (2026), deposited by the author of this page without peer review, borrows from the sociologist Pierre Bourdieu the notion of the academic field: a space of struggles for the monopoly of legitimate scientific competence. It puts forward the hypothesis that in the social sciences, and in economics above all, this field maintains its order — criteria of validity, publication formats, hierarchies of legitimacy — by **relegating to its margins** certain anomalies: questions, formats, postures. It tests this on a single case, in economics, and warns: "A single case does not establish a law."

Not every marginality is a displaced cost, AWP-05 specifies: one speaks of **transfer** only when three conditions combine — the contribution is ignored without being refuted, no mechanism reassesses it, and its cost falls on identifiable actors. According to AWP-05, open infrastructures do not remove this logic: they reduce its cost, and the transfer changes form — from silent relegation to drowning in noise, when everything becomes accessible and sorting falls to the reader — not nature. Publishing has become easy; being **read and discussed** remains the test.

AWP-05 itself sets a limit to this reading: the framework "cannot serve as a self-sufficient explanation of its own marginality without sinking into circularity", and it has to be tested on observable phenomena, independently of its author's position. The case that follows illustrates how the tools are used; it does not prove the hypothesis.

## The concrete case

This page is written from the position it describes. Stéphane Lalut, an economist by training, works as an independent researcher and essayist: nine working papers with DOIs ([AWP series](/en/serie-awp/)), six published books, reviews he has written for social science journals such as *Sociologie du travail* and the *Revue française de socio-économie*, and articles and reviews in *Alternatives Économiques*, *La Vie des Idées*, *En attendant Nadeau*, *Terrestres*, *Nonfiction*, *Le Temps* or *La Grande Conversation*, as well as posts on his blog at the *Mediapart* Club ([publications](/publications/) — in French). Public identifiers: [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-1794-4895), [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=J4NqzwSfrHAC), [Zenodo community](https://zenodo.org/communities/anthropie-working-papers). His books, articles and working papers are gathered on the [About](/en/a-propos/) page.
