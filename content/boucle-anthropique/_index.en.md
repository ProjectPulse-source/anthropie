The anthropic loop is formalised by economist Stéphane Lalut in the working paper [AWP-07](/en/awp/awp-07/) (2026; the page follows its revised version, not yet deposited, and the version deposited in July 2026 differs from it on several points, including whether accumulation is automatic), in explicit dialogue with K. William Kapp's *cost-shifting* (shifting costs onto third parties), Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's bioeconomics and Bernard Stiegler's neganthropy.

## The five moments of the loop

According to the hypothesis of [anthropy](/en/quest-ce-que-lanthropie/), a social system — an institution, an economy, a territory — produces its order by exporting disorder. The loop follows that disorder through five states. Displacement opens the loop; none of the later states is compulsory: the load may never accumulate, the receptacle may not saturate, and no return may occur within the period studied.

1. **E1 — Displacement**: disorder is exported toward a receptacle — a spatial, temporal or social elsewhere; the three axes can combine.
2. **E2 — Accumulation**: the load accumulates if inflows exceed what the receptacle absorbs or regenerates; otherwise it can stabilise or decline.
3. **E3 — Saturation**: the receptacle reaches a capacity limit, or contestation imposes an effective constraint on the continuation of the transfer.
4. **E4 — Return**: disorder may come back toward the emitting system — rarely in the form in which it was exported.
5. **E5 — Bifurcation**: the system re-displaces toward a new receptacle, reconfigures itself, or partly collapses.

The diagram also carries three paths outside the sequence. **Transfer arrest** leads straight from accumulation to bifurcation: the emitter stops displacing before any saturation, when the transfer costs it more than it brings in. The **early return** toward the emitter occurs during displacement or accumulation — leaks, immediate backlash — without changing the state of the loop. Finally, at every passage, **counter-tendencies** — regeneration, repayment, technical substitution, a political reaction — can delay, deflect or prevent what follows; and the load can be internalised: the system deals with it at home, and the loop stops.

### One full turn: asbestos

The asbestos industry produced its order by making others bear a risk (E1): the bodies of workers and local residents, on the social axis, and the building stock. The load accumulated out of sight (E2): the latency of mesothelioma, a cancer most often of the pleura, is "often of the order of 30 to 40 years", according to the French Senate's 2005 fact-finding mission. Saturation did not come from a lack of room but from contestation (E3): lawsuits, the press and independent expertise wore down the doubt the industry had kept alive. The return took a different form from the one that left (E4): as spending on compensation and asbestos removal. It reached the emitting firms only in part: its burden fell mostly, according to the Senate in 2005, on all the employers of the general social-security scheme, including those that had never used asbestos. The outcome (E5) was forced internalisation of the use — not of the inherited costs, which remain partly pooled —, through the national ban of 1997; whether it opened another, through use displaced to countries that did not yet ban asbestos, is not established by the sources used here.

### When turns follow one another

For one turn to open another, the outcome must be a new displacement: the emitter has changed, and the cycle starts again from another configuration. That is when the loop becomes a **spiral**. AWP-07 rereads in this way the energy transition analysed by [AWP-04](/en/awp/awp-04/). The fossil regime shifted part of its costs into the atmosphere, and so into the future; the response to its saturation opens a new circuit, spatial and material — lithium from Chile, cobalt from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, far from the centres of consumption — to which the [rebound effect](/en/rebound-effect/) adds a new load. Yet the “Environment” button on the figure shows that this new turn does not replace the old one: the fossil regime remains an active strand there, with the transition coiling around it, because energy sources add up more than they substitute for one another. Each re-displacement wears down the available elsewheres a little more, unless they replenish fast enough: a turn is therefore not necessarily worse than the one before. That these turns nest, the saturation of one level spreading to the level above, from territory to world-system, remains a research hypothesis: a drift observed over time, at a single level, does not demonstrate it.

## Kapp: cost-shifting is systemic

The first lineage is economic. As early as 1950, in *The Social Costs of Private Enterprise*, K. William Kapp established that shifting costs onto third parties is not an accidental market failure but a systemic practice: private enterprise is structurally incentivised to move part of its costs onto workers, neighbours, the environment, the future. The anthropic loop extends Kapp on two points: it generalises the mechanism to *any* order-producing system — not only the firm — and it follows what becomes of it: the displaced cost does not vanish into a memoryless elsewhere, and the loop asks under what conditions it accumulates, saturates its receptacle and returns.

## Georgescu-Roegen: there is no infinite elsewhere

The second lineage comes from bioeconomics. In *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process* (1971), Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen argued that no economic process cancels disorder: every production of local order is paid for by degradation somewhere. The anthropic loop keeps this thesis as background — the idea that a "displacement without remainder" does not exist — without importing thermodynamics: its receptacles are not energy reservoirs but **social, territorial and attentional** spaces, and its saturation is read in institutions, not in joules. The lesson retained is that of the closed system: there is no infinite elsewhere.

## Stiegler: an acknowledged homonymy

The third lineage is also a demarcation. Bernard Stiegler used the word "anthropy" for human-generated entropy — the share of disorder that human activities inject into living and technical systems — and opposed to it **neganthropy**: the forms of knowledge, care and bifurcation capable of producing local negentropy. That is a grammar of *quantities*: produce less anthropy, more neganthropy.

Anthropy in Lalut's sense belongs to another grammar: it does not measure a production, it traces a **route**. The question is not "how much disorder do we produce?" but "where does disorder go, who absorbs it, and through which mediations does its route become invisible?". [AWP-07](/en/awp/awp-07/) fixes this acknowledged homonymy — same word, two concepts — and, in passing, returns the credit of the "boomerang effect" to Ulrich Beck, while demarcating the loop from its conceptual neighbours: David Harvey's *spatio-temporal fix*, Stephan Lessenich's externalisation society, system dynamics.

## The apparatus's own contribution: invisibility as a cost, return as transmutation

Two pieces of the apparatus carry its added value. The first is the third saturation condition, **C3**: the invisibility of a transfer is not a state of affairs but a **maintenance cost** — keeping a displacement out of sight requires spending, in devices and narratives — and this cost is observable *before* the return. The second is the **transmutation of the return**: disorder rarely comes back in the form in which it was exported — an ecological cost displaced may return as a health cost, an export of matter as a political issue. That this change of form makes a return harder to attribute to its original displacement is a hypothesis, which the paper proposes to test, not a result.

## A formalisation built to be refuted

The formalisation — nine definitions, five states, three conditions, four forms of return, six propositions with differentiated status — has value only if it increases refutability. AWP-07 sets an *ex ante* protocol: name the receptacle and the order of magnitude of the latency **before** the analysis, so that one can neither assert an untraceable displacement nor certify a complacent internalisation. One case external to the corpus — asbestos — is coded in full to show how the grid is used, not to validate it.
