The Register of Offloaded Costs is the **documentary apparatus** of the book [*ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization's Hidden Costs*](/en/livres/anthropie-ordre-ici-dette-ailleurs/): the book tells deep history in 168 milestones, from the first stone tools (≈ 3.3 million years ago) to contemporary algorithms, and the Register asks each of them the same two questions: **what order did this innovation create, and what debt did it displace**—toward other places, other times, or other social groups? The English version, below, is freely browsable; it holds all 168 milestones of the book.

## How to read a milestone

Each entry applies the grid of the anthropic hypothesis—[social systems displace disorder rather than resolve it](/en/quest-ce-que-lanthropie/):

- **Order created**—the visible, immediate benefit, the structure gained: food security, written memory, monetary trust, coordination at a distance;
- **Debt displaced**—the invisible cost that this order moves out of its visible perimeter: material dependence, social exclusion, charges passed on to later generations;
- **Categories**—the nature of the transfer: energetic, social, cognitive, political, ecological, symbolic…

The Register does not tell the story of progress: it keeps its **accounts**. What other narratives present as solutions appears here as displacements—every solution incurs a debt, and history is the sequence of these transfers.

## The trajectory: from the spatial to the cognitive

Read end to end, the chronology draws a migration that the book's afterword sums up. In **ages 1 to 3**, order displaces disorder **spatially**: the dead beyond the village, waste beyond the city, "barbarians" beyond the empire. In **ages 4 and 5**, **socially**: onto serfs, onto colonies, onto labouring classes. In **ages 6 and 7**, **temporally and cognitively**: onto future generations (climate), onto attention (algorithms). Each time a receptacle saturates, the escape reinvents itself elsewhere—more abstract, more distant, less politicizable.

This dynamic—displacement, accumulation, saturation, return—is formalized in [AWP-07, *The Anthropic Loop*](/en/awp/awp-07/); the deep-history reading of the Register is the object of [AWP-02, *3.3 Million Years in One Principle*](/en/awp/awp-02/).

## What the Register does not say

The Register shows **where the debts go**; it does not show how they come back. Yet they do come back—metamorphosed: CO₂ as heatwaves, colonial extraction as migrations, captured attention as dependencies. That return is precisely the object of the [anthropic loop](/en/anthropic-loop/), formalized in [AWP-07](/en/awp/awp-07/).

Another limit, assumed by the book: the Register's categories—economic, social, political—are themselves analytical tools that carve up reality, and that carving can serve the very displacement it describes: separating the economic from the social makes it possible to move costs from one to the other. The Register records; it does not claim to settle what part of each transfer was avoidable.

## Where this register comes from

[*ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization's Hidden Costs*](/en/livres/anthropie-ordre-ici-dette-ailleurs/) (2026, 644 pages) develops each milestone as a chapter, sums up each age in an "order created / disorder displaced" diagram, and draws the overall trajectory in its afterword; it points to this page through a QR code ("Going Further," p. 631). The Register condenses each chapter onto the common grid, in the author’s words; milestones are organized by the seven ages of the book, according to their chronological bounds. When the book is corrected, the Register follows: revisions are logged at the foot of the page. The working vocabulary of the framework is defined in the [glossary](/en/glossaire/)—not to be confused with the [coupled cost registers](/en/glossaire/#coupled-cost-registers) of AWP-06, a typology rather than a chronology. The French version is browsable at [Le Registre des coûts déportés](/registre-des-couts-deportes/).
