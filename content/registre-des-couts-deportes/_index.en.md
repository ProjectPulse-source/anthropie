The Register of Deported Costs is the **documentary apparatus** of the book [*ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization's Hidden Costs*](/en/livres/anthropie-ordre-ici-dette-ailleurs/): a chronology of 168 milestones, from the first stone tools (≈ 3.3 million years ago) to contemporary algorithms. Each milestone answers the same two questions: **what order did this innovation create, and what debt did it deport** — toward other places, other times or other social groups? It is the accounting of the costs each civilization displaced to sustain its local order.

## How to read a milestone

Each entry applies the grid of the anthropic hypothesis — [social systems displace disorder rather than resolve it](/en/quest-ce-que-lanthropie/):

- **Order created** — what the innovation stabilises: food security, written memory, monetary trust, coordination at a distance;
- **Debt deported** — the cost that this order displaces out of its visible perimeter: material dependence, social exclusion, charges passed on to later generations;
- **Categories** — the nature of the transfer: energetic, social, cognitive, political, ecological, symbolic…

Three examples, adapted from the Register. *The first stone tools* (≈ 3.3 million years ago): memory and intention externalised into durable artefacts — at the price of a dependence on materials and know-how, a cognitive cost converted into a thermodynamic one. *The Neolithic* (≈ 10,000 BCE): food security through surplus and settlement — at the price of bondage to land and labour, storage-based hierarchies, an agricultural debt owed to soils and climate. *Global financial markets* (2024): liquidity and efficiency of capital flows — at the price of displaced disorder onto peoples, through austerity and precarity, to guarantee the stability of assets.

The Register does not tell the story of progress: it keeps its **accounts**. What other narratives present as solutions appears here as displacements — every solution incurs a debt, and history is the sequence of these transfers.

## The seven ages

1. **The founding programs** (≈ 3.3 million years ago → 10,000 BCE)
2. **The Neolithic trap** (10,000 → 1,000 BCE)
3. **The operating systems of antiquity** (1,000 BCE → 500 CE)
4. **Networks: roads, relays, relays of power** (500 → 1500)
5. **The modern state-machine** (1500 → 1800)
6. **Industrialization and nations** (1800 → 1945)
7. **The contemporary metaprogram** (1945 → present)

Read end to end, the chronology draws a migration that the book's afterword sums up. In **ages 1 to 3**, order displaces disorder **spatially** — the dead outside the village, waste outside the city, « barbarians » outside the empire. In **ages 4 and 5**, **socially** — onto serfs, onto colonies, onto labouring classes. In **ages 6 and 7**, **temporally and cognitively** — onto future generations (climate), onto attention (algorithms). Each time a receptacle saturates, the escape reinvents itself elsewhere: more abstract, more distant, less politicisable.

This dynamic — displacement, accumulation, saturation, return — is formalised in [AWP-07 — *The Anthropic Loop*](/en/awp/awp-07/); the deep-history reading of the Register is the object of [AWP-02 — *3.3 Million Years in One Principle*](/en/awp/awp-02/).

## What the Register does not say

The Register shows **where the debts go**; it does not show how they come back. Yet they do come back — metamorphosed: CO₂ as heatwaves, colonial extraction as migrations, captured attention as dependencies. That return is precisely the object of the [anthropic loop](/en/anthropic-loop/), formalised in [AWP-07](/en/awp/awp-07/).

Another limit, assumed by the book: the Register's categories — economic, social, political — are themselves analytical tools that carve up reality, and that carving can serve the very deportation it describes: separating the economic from the social makes it possible to displace costs from one to the other. The Register records; it does not claim to settle what part of each transfer was avoidable.

## Where to find it

- The complete Register in English forms the epilogue of [*ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization's Hidden Costs*](/en/livres/anthropie-ordre-ici-dette-ailleurs/) (2026, 632 p.), alongside the diagrams and the analytical afterword.
- The French online version is freely browsable, milestone by milestone and anchored entry by entry: [Le Registre des coûts déportés](/registre-des-couts-deportes/).
- The working vocabulary of the framework is defined in the [glossary](/en/glossaire/) — not to be confused with the [coupled cost registers](/en/glossaire/#coupled-cost-registers) of AWP-06, a typology rather than a chronology.
