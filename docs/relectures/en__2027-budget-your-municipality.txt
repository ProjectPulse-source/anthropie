Budget 2027: what do we actually know for your municipality?
In plenary, on 19 October 2026, the National Assembly deleted the cut in the FCTVA rate (art.
35) and the progressive contribution to the budgetary effort (art.
37), did not adopt the capping of assigned VAT (art.
36): three of the five measures the bill as tabled aimed at local authorities, including the two largest by amount, are no longer in the text it is examining.
Nothing is settled: the procedure goes on (the first part continues to be examined from 27 October to 2 November 2026, with a formal vote on the first part on 3 November), the Senate will follow, and a deleted measure may be reinstated.
No municipal amount can be inferred from a bill; amounts will come from the act as passed, then from the notifications and orders this page lists measure by measure.

This page is revised at each step that changes these measures; its revisions remain readable at the foot of the page.
A mayor preparing the budget orientation debate reads two things at once that do not say the same thing: the amounts announced when the bill was presented, and what Parliament has done with them since.
This page keeps the two apart.
For each measure that affects municipalities, it answers four questions: what did it provide for, what did the National Assembly decide, what may follow for a municipality, and when will more be known.

The arithmetic sum of these five amounts, €6.2 billion, described the bill as tabled; it did not measure a net levy, and the page on local authorities explains why it coexisted with a projected rise in local resources.
Since 19 October 2026, it no longer describes the text under discussion.
An article deleted or not adopted in plenary is no longer in the text under discussion, but it may come back, through a Government amendment or in the Senate.
When the debate opened on 19 October, the minister for local authorities, Françoise Gatel, put the contribution of local authorities provided for by the bill at €5.5 billion.
Measure by measure
The progressive contribution to the budgetary effort (article 37)
What the bill provided for.
A contribution of €2.5 billion for 2027, owed by 40% of municipalities according to the explanatory memorandum.
Municipalities are ranked on an index that weights their financial potential per inhabitant at 75% and income per inhabitant at 25%, each relative to the average.
The rate runs from 0.5% for the first decile to 2% for the tenth, and 3% for the top two percentiles.
It applies to the real operating revenue of the main budget of the year before last, that is 2025, net in particular of revenue reductions and exceptional revenue.
Exempt are municipalities in the overseas departments and regions, those that article L.
2336-3 of the General Code of Local Authorities already exempts from the inter-municipal equalisation fund (the first 250 municipalities of 10,000 inhabitants or more and the first 30 of 5,000 to 9,999 in the ranking for the urban solidarity grant, the first 2,500 in the ranking for the targeted share of the rural solidarity grant), and those whose contribution would be under €5,000.
Each municipality’s amount was to be notified by an order in the Journal officiel, then deducted every month from the monthly tax advances: a cash effect from notification onwards, before it even shows in the budget.
What the National Assembly decided.
State on 22 October 2026: art.
37 deleted by the National Assembly on 19 October 2026.
What may follow for a municipality.
Nothing is owed as the text stands.
Were the article reinstated unchanged, a non-exempt municipality could place its contribution between 0.5% and 3% of its 2025 real operating revenue, after deductions: a static comparison, not an amount.
The exact rate depends on its rank among all municipalities, which several consultancies and associations of elected officials have simulated on the initial text; those simulations describe the article as tabled, and the associations that publish them have called for it to be deleted or reduced.
When more will be known.
At the formal vote on the first part, then in the Senate.
Were the contribution reinstated, each municipality’s amount would be the one in the order published in the Journal officiel.
The VAT compensation fund (articles 35 and 41)
What the bill provided for.
For payments made from 1 January 2027, a rate cut from 16.404% to 11.404%, that is 5 percentage points lower, except for spending on the ecological transition and on roads, kept at 16.404%, and a yield of €2.1 billion.
The list of accounts qualifying for the maintained rate was left to a forthcoming order.
What the National Assembly decided.
State on 22 October 2026: art.
35 deleted by the National Assembly on 19 October 2026.
What may follow for a municipality.
The measure bore on payments made, not on spending committed.
Under article L.
1615-6 of the Code, the payments of a municipality under the standard regime relate to spending of the year before last: in 2027, spending of 2025, already carried out at the former rate.
For some municipalities, payments relate to the previous year’s spending; for new municipalities, to the current year’s.
For the share of spending that would have fallen under the reduced rate, 5 points out of 16.404 amount to a little under a third of the corresponding payment.
Without the split of its spending between the maintained and reduced rates, which depended on the order, a municipality cannot derive an amount.
When more will be known.
When the act is passed, then, if the measure came back, when the order setting the accounts at the maintained rate is published.
The shares of VAT (article 36)
What the bill provided for.
For the shares of VAT paid in place of abolished taxes (article 16 of the 2020 budget act and article 55 of the 2023 act), annual growth reduced by the previous year’s inflation excluding tobacco, without the amount being allowed to fall from one year to the next.
The High Council of Public Finance put the capping at €0.6 billion for all recipients.
What the National Assembly decided.
State on 22 October 2026: art.
36 not adopted by the National Assembly on 19 October 2026.
The amendments deleting the article had first been rejected; it is the amended article that was not adopted.
What may follow for a municipality.
Only authorities receiving such a share are concerned; the effect would have depended on VAT receipts and on inflation, unknown to date.
Not determinable for a municipality.
When more will be known.
When the act is passed.
The general operating grant (articles 34 and 83)
What the bill provided for.
A national amount of €27.5 billion; a rise in the urban and rural solidarity grants funded partly by capping the lump-sum grant (article 83, second part).
The general presentation of the bill and the explanatory memorandum to article 83 do not split that rise the same way between the two grants: the Local Finance Committee will set it.
What the National Assembly decided.
Article 34 adopted as amended: the DGF for 2027 is raised to €27.9 billion, €356 million more than in the bill.
It is an amount for 2027, not an indexation rule.
Article 83 belongs to the second part, which the National Assembly has not yet examined.
What may follow for a municipality.
A national amount is not shared out among municipalities in simple proportion: each grant follows its own criteria, and a municipality may see its lump-sum grant capped while its solidarity grant rises.
Not determinable before the distribution.
When more will be known.
When individual allocations are notified, after the budget act is passed and the Local Finance Committee has decided.
The sums placed in reserve in 2025 and 2026 (DILICO, article 84)
What the bill provided for.
Repaying over five years, in fifths from 2027 to 2031, the remaining two thirds of the first scheme and all of the second, instead of over three years in thirds; according to the explanatory memorandum, about 2,000 authorities are concerned and the total amount returned does not change.
What the National Assembly decided.
State on 22 October 2026: second part, not yet examined.
What may follow for a municipality.
For a contributing municipality, the calculation is exact: from the amount placed in reserve, notified to it by an order, the annual payment would fall from a third to a fifth.
The effect is on cash flow, not on the amount.
When more will be known.
When the second part is examined, then at the single repayment order the bill provides for.
Other provisions
Grant for low-density rural municipalities (article 85, second part): €50 million, reserved for municipalities classed as sparsely or very sparsely populated in the Insee grid whose density is below a reference average, excluding recipients of the rural amenities grant, and shared out by area under a forthcoming decree.
Post-parking charges (article 53, first part, whose examination continues): 1.9% of charges paid after an enforcement order is issued would go to the central government budget, for orders issued from 1 January 2027.
Greater Paris Metropolis (article 40): the extension of the transitional financing arrangement to 2028 was not adopted on 19 October 2026; the metropolis’s municipalities are concerned through their territorial public body.
When will the municipality know?
In Parliament: the first part continues to be examined from 27 October to 2 November 2026, with a formal vote on the first part on 3 November; then the Senate, and final adoption.
After the act: the orders and decrees it provides for (contribution, FCTVA accounts, distribution of the new rural grant), then notification of grants.
The initial budget must be adopted before 15 April.
Under article L.
1612-2 of the Code, that deadline does not apply when the delay results from information essential to drawing up the budget not being communicated before 31 March; the council then has fifteen days after that communication.
A municipality may thus have to vote its budget before receiving all the amounts the State must notify to it: the exception in article L.
1612-2 exists for that case.
For finance departments, this lag matters as much as the amount of the measures.
What the municipality can look at now
Its own 2025 accounts, published by the Observatory of Local Public Finance and Management (OFGL) on data.ofgl.fr: real operating revenue, the base of the contribution if it came back; FCTVA received; the DGF; gross savings, which show the margin available to absorb a fall in revenue.
The page on local authorities explains how to read these lines.
What this page does not say
It does not forecast the final text: a measure deleted in plenary may be reinstated.
It gives no amount for any municipality; the orders of magnitude it mentions are static comparisons, under the conditions written next to them.
It covers only the provisions of the bill that target municipalities directly; general tax measures may also weigh on their revenue.

Frequently asked questions
Does the progressive contribution to the budgetary effort (CPEB) apply to my municipality?
Not at this stage: article 37, which created it, was deleted in plenary by the National Assembly on 19 October 2026.
The budget procedure is not over and the article may be reinstated.
If it were reinstated as tabled, the contribution of a non-exempt municipality would lie between 0.5% and 3% of its 2025 real operating revenue, after deductions; its exact amount would be set by a ministerial order published in the Journal officiel.
Does the cut in the FCTVA rate affect investment already carried out?
In the bill as tabled, the new rate applied to payments made from 1 January 2027.
For a municipality under the standard regime, those payments relate to spending of the year before last: in 2027, spending of 2025, already carried out.
Article 35 was deleted in plenary by the National Assembly on 19 October 2026; it may be reinstated before the final act.
When will my municipality know its DGF for 2027?
After the budget act is passed, through the notification of its individual allocations.
The national amount voted by the National Assembly in the first part, €27.9 billion, says nothing about any municipality: the distribution depends on the criteria of each grant and on the choices of the Local Finance Committee.
