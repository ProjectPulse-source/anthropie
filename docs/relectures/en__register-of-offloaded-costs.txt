The Register of Offloaded Costs
Page updated 1 November 2026 · revisions
The Register of Offloaded Costs is the documentary apparatus of the book ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization’s Hidden Costs: the book tells deep history in 168 milestones, from the first stone tools (≈ 3.3 million years ago) to contemporary algorithms, and the Register asks each of them the same two questions: what order did this innovation create, and what debt did it displace—toward other places, other times, or other social groups?
The English version, below, is freely browsable; it holds all 168 milestones of the book.

How to read a milestone
Each entry applies the grid of the anthropic hypothesis—social systems displace disorder rather than resolve it:
Order created—the visible, immediate benefit, the structure gained: food security, written memory, monetary trust, coordination at a distance;
Debt displaced—the invisible cost that this order moves out of its visible perimeter: material dependence, social exclusion, charges passed on to later generations;
Categories—the nature of the transfer: energetic, social, cognitive, political, ecological, symbolic…
The idea in its history—for 68 milestones, the stop in L’Odyssée des idées (in French) that deals with the same idea, thinker or event: the Register keeps the account of its displaced cost, the stop tells its history.
The Register does not tell the story of progress: it keeps its accounts.
What other narratives present as solutions appears here as displacements—every solution incurs a debt, and history is the sequence of these transfers.

The trajectory: from the spatial to the cognitive
Read end to end, the chronology draws a migration that the book’s afterword sums up.
In ages 1 to 3, order displaces disorder spatially: the dead beyond the village, waste beyond the city, “barbarians” beyond the empire.
In ages 4 and 5, socially: onto serfs, onto colonies, onto labouring classes.
In ages 6 and 7, temporally and cognitively: onto future generations (climate), onto attention (algorithms).
Each time a receptacle saturates, the escape reinvents itself elsewhere—more abstract, more distant, less politicizable.
This dynamic—displacement, accumulation, saturation, return—is formalized in AWP-07, The Anthropic Loop; the deep-history reading of the Register is the object of AWP-02, 3.3 Million Years in One Principle.
What the Register does not say
The Register shows where the debts go; it does not show how they come back.
Yet they do come back—metamorphosed: CO₂ as heatwaves, colonial extraction as migrations, captured attention as dependencies.
That return is precisely the object of the anthropic loop, formalized in AWP-07.
Another limit, assumed by the book: the Register’s categories—economic, social, political—are themselves analytical tools that carve up reality, and that carving can serve the very displacement it describes: separating the economic from the social makes it possible to move costs from one to the other.
The Register records; it does not claim to settle what part of each transfer was avoidable.

Frequently asked questions
What is the Register of Offloaded Costs?
The Register of Offloaded Costs is the documentary apparatus of the book ANTHROPY – A Big History of Civilization’s Hidden Costs (Stéphane Lalut, English edition 2026; French original ANTHROPIE, 2025): the book tells deep history in 168 milestones, from the first stone tools (≈ 3.3 million years ago) to contemporary algorithms, and the Register brings each milestone down to the same grid: the Order created by an innovation and the Debt displaced—the disorder moved toward other places, other times, or other groups.
The book points to it through a QR code; all 168 milestones are freely browsable on this page.
What are the seven ages of anthropy?
The book organizes deep history into seven ages: 1.
Foundational Programs (8 milestones, ≈ 3.3 million years ago → ≈ 45,000 BCE); 2.
The Neolithic Trap (7, ≈ 10,000 → ≈ 1046 BCE); 3.
The Extractive Empires (34, ≈ 900 BCE → 312 CE); 4.
The Networks—Routes, Relays, Relays of Power (20, 486 → 1494); 5.
The Modern State-machine (24, 1513 → 1784); 6.
Industrialization and Nation-States (29, 1789 → 1905); 7.
The Contemporary Metaprogram (46, 1905 → 2024).
The book sets no boundaries for its ages: each is defined by its chapters, and the Register places each milestone in the age of its chapter.
What do “Order created” and “Debt displaced” mean?
It is the reading grid of every milestone in the Register.
The “Order created” is the visible, immediate benefit, the structure gained—food security, written memory, monetary trust, coordination at a distance.
The “Debt displaced” is the invisible cost, exported in time, in space, or onto other groups.
Categories (energetic, social, cognitive, political…) specify the nature of each transfer.
The grid applies the anthropic hypothesis: order is not created, it is paid for elsewhere.
What is the oldest transfer documented in the Register?
The first stone tools, around 3.3 million years ago.
Order created: memory and intention externalized into durable artifacts, creating transmissible knowledge independent of its bearer.
Debt displaced: dependence on materials and know-how, a cognitive cost converted into a thermodynamic cost of extraction and shaping.
The displacement of disorder therefore precedes industrialization by a very long way.
How has the displacement of disorder evolved across the seven ages?
The book’s afterword draws a clear trajectory.
Ages 1 to 3: order displaces disorder spatially—the dead beyond the village, waste beyond the city, “barbarians” beyond the empire.
Ages 4 and 5: socially—onto serfs, colonies, labouring classes.
Ages 6 and 7: temporally and cognitively—onto future generations (climate), onto attention (algorithms).
Each time a receptacle saturates, the escape reinvents itself elsewhere: more abstract, more distant, less politicizable.
Is the Register of Offloaded Costs the same thing as the coupled cost registers?
No.
The Register of Offloaded Costs is the documentary apparatus of the book ANTHROPY: a chronology of the book’s historical milestones.
The coupled cost registers are a theoretical contribution of AWP-06: the grid of four registers—energy, matter, territory, attention—along which digital infrastructure simultaneously displaces disorder.
One is a chronology, the other a typology; only the word “register” is shared.
